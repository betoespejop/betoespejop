# Daily English – 2026-11-15

## Phrasal verbs

### 1. Sort out
**Meaning:** to fix a problem, organise something, or deal with a situation.
**Contexts:** very common in Australia — at work, with bills, with paperwork, with plans.
- "Don't worry about the internet, I'll **sort it out** with the provider tomorrow."
- "I need to **sort out** my tax file number before I start the new job."
- "Have you **sorted out** where we're meeting on Saturday?"

### 2. Pick up
**Meaning:** (a) to collect someone or something; (b) to learn something informally; (c) to improve.
**Contexts:** transport/errands, learning a language, business or weather getting better.
- "Can you **pick me up** from the station at 6?" (collect)
- "I **picked up** a lot of Aussie slang from my housemates." (learn)
- "Sales usually **pick up** before Christmas." (improve)

### 3. Put off
**Meaning:** to postpone / delay something, often because you don't want to do it.
**Contexts:** appointments, tasks, decisions, procrastination.
- "I keep **putting off** going to the dentist."
- "The meeting was **put off** until next week because of the storm."
- "Stop **putting it off** — just call the landlord!"
- Tip: followed by a verb → use **-ing**: "put off doing something".

## Verbs

| Verb | Type | Past simple | Past participle | -ing |
|------|------|-------------|-----------------|------|
| spend | irregular | spent | spent | spending |
| lend | irregular | lent | lent | lending |
| borrow | regular | borrowed | borrowed | borrowing |
| book | regular | booked | booked | booking |

### Spend (irregular)
**Meaning:** to use money to buy things, or to use time doing something.
- "I **spent** $80 on groceries at Woolies yesterday."
- "We've **spent** the whole weekend at the beach."

### Lend (irregular)
**Meaning:** to give something to someone temporarily (you are the owner).
- "Could you **lend** me your charger? Mine is dead."
- "She has **lent** me her car twice this month."

### Borrow (regular)
**Meaning:** to take something from someone temporarily (they are the owner). Opposite direction of *lend*.
- "Can I **borrow** your umbrella? It's pouring outside."
- "I **borrowed** three books from the local library."
- Tip: **lend TO** someone / **borrow FROM** someone.

### Book (regular)
**Meaning:** to reserve something in advance (table, ticket, appointment, room).
- "I've **booked** a table for four at 7:30."
- "Are you **booking** the flights to Melbourne tonight?"
