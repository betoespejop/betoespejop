# Daily English – 2026-11-22

## Phrasal verb: Top up

**Meaning:** to add more of something so it's full again, usually money or credit (or a drink).

**Contexts (very Aussie!):** Opal/Myki card, phone credit, petrol, someone's drink, savings.

- "I need to **top up** my Opal card before I catch the train."
- "My phone credit ran out, so I **topped it up** online."
- "Can I **top up** your wine?" (= pour more)
- Noun: "I did a $20 **top-up** on my phone."

**Easy tip:** *top* = the top of a glass → you fill it *up* to the top again.

## Verbs

| Verb | Type | Present | Past simple | Past participle | -ing |
|------|------|---------|-------------|-----------------|------|
| speak | irregular | speak / speaks | **spoke** | **spoken** | speaking |
| stay | regular | stay / stays | **stayed** | **stayed** | staying |

### speak (irregular) – to say words; to use a language
- Present: "I **speak** Spanish and a bit of English."
- Past: "I **spoke** to my manager about my roster yesterday."
- Present perfect: "Have you **spoken** to the landlord yet?"
- Continuous: "Sorry, I'm **speaking** on the phone right now."
- Future: "I **will speak** to her tomorrow."

**Easy tip:** sp**ea**k → sp**o**ke → sp**o**ken (like *break → broke → broken*).

### stay (regular) – to remain in a place; to live somewhere for a short time
- Present: "I **stay** home on Sundays to relax."
- Past: "We **stayed** in a hostel in Melbourne for a week."
- Present perfect: "I've **stayed** in Australia for six months now."
- Continuous: "I'm **staying** with friends in Bondi this month."
- Future: "I'm going to **stay** late at work tonight."

**Easy tip:** vowel + *y* → just add *-ed*: stay**ed** (not "staied").
