# Daily English – 2026-11-22

## Phrasal verb: Pick up
**Meaning:**
1. To collect someone or something from a place (by car, on foot).
2. To lift something from the floor/table.
3. To learn something naturally, without formal study.

**Contexts:** driving/transport, shopping & takeaway, learning English in Australia.
- "Can you **pick me up** from the train station at 6?" (collect someone)
- "I'll **pick up** a coffee on the way to work." (collect/buy something)
- "Please **pick up** your clothes from the floor." (lift)
- "I'm **picking up** a lot of Aussie slang at work." (learn naturally)

💡 Remember: *pick* = choose + *up* = lift → you lift something/someone and take it with you.

## Verbs

| Verb | Type | Present (he/she) | Past simple | Past participle | -ing |
|------|------|------------------|-------------|-----------------|------|
| take | irregular | takes | took | taken | taking |
| book | regular | books | booked | booked | booking |

### take (irregular) – to carry/move something, or to use transport/time.
- Present: "I **take** the bus to work every day."
- Past: "I **took** the ferry to Manly yesterday."
- Present perfect: "Have you **taken** your medicine?"
- Continuous: "She's **taking** an English course."
- Future: "It **will take** 20 minutes to get there."

### book (regular) – to reserve something in advance (table, ticket, appointment).
- Present: "I always **book** my flights online."
- Past: "I **booked** a table for two at 7pm."
- Present perfect: "Have you **booked** the doctor's appointment?"
- Continuous: "We're **booking** a trip to Melbourne."
- Future: "I'**ll book** the tickets tonight."

💡 Mini-story: *"I **booked** a tour, **took** the train to the city, and a friend **picked me up** at the station."*
