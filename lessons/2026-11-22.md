# Daily English – 2026-11-22

## Phrasal verb: Go out
**Meaning:**
1. To leave your home to do something social (eat, drink, party, see a movie).
2. To be in a romantic relationship with someone ("go out with").
3. (A light or fire) to stop working/burning.

**Contexts:** weekends, friends, dating, power cuts.
- "Do you want to **go out** for dinner on Friday?" (social)
- "We **went out** to a pub in the city last night." (social)
- "Tom is **going out with** a girl from his English class." (dating)
- "The lights **went out** during the storm." (stopped working)

💡 Remember: *go* + *out* (of the house) → you leave home to have fun.

## Verbs

| Verb | Type | Present (he/she) | Past simple | Past participle | -ing |
|------|------|------------------|-------------|-----------------|------|
| sleep | irregular | sleeps | slept | slept | sleeping |
| help | regular | helps | helped | helped | helping |

### sleep (irregular) – to rest with your eyes closed, not awake.
- Present: "I **sleep** eight hours every night."
- Past: "I **slept** badly because of the heat."
- Present perfect: "Have you **slept** well?"
- Continuous: "Shh, the baby is **sleeping**."
- Future: "I **will sleep** at my friend's place tonight."

### help (regular) – to make something easier for someone; to give support.
- Present: "My flatmate always **helps** me with my homework."
- Past: "A stranger **helped** me find the bus stop."
- Present perfect: "This app **has helped** me a lot with my English."
- Continuous: "I'm **helping** my boss at the café today."
- Future: "I **will help** you move on Saturday."

💡 Mini-story: *"We **went out** late, so I only **slept** five hours. Coffee **helped** me survive work!"*
