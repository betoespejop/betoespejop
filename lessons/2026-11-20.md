# Daily English – 2026-11-20

## Phrasal verbs

### 1. Find out
**Meaning:** to discover a fact or piece of information, often by asking, reading or checking.
**Context:** timetables, prices, news, results, information about someone. (Use *find* for a physical object; *find out* for information.)
- "Can you **find out** what time the last train leaves Central?"
- "I just **found out** my visa application was approved!"
- "She went online to **find out** how much a rental bond costs."

### 2. Hold on
**Meaning:** (a) to wait for a short time; (b) to grip something tightly so you don't fall.
**Context:** phone calls, conversations, customer service, public transport (buses, trams).
- "**Hold on** a sec, I'll grab my wallet." (very common with *a sec / a minute*)
- "Please **hold on** while I transfer your call."
- "**Hold on** tight — the bus driver brakes hard on this road!"

### 3. Head off
**Meaning:** to leave a place to go somewhere else. Very common in Australian English.
**Context:** saying goodbye at work, parties, the pub, starting a trip.
- "Right, I'm gonna **head off** now — see you tomorrow!"
- "We're **heading off** to the beach early, around 7."
- "What time did they **head off** last night?"

## Verbs

| Verb | Type | Present | Past simple | Past participle | -ing form |
|------|------|---------|-------------|-----------------|-----------|
| meet | irregular | meet / meets | met | met | meeting |
| sell | irregular | sell / sells | sold | sold | selling |
| carry | regular | carry / carries | carried | carried | carrying |
| fix | regular | fix / fixes | fixed | fixed | fixing |

- **meet** – (a) to see and talk to someone for the first time; (b) to come together with someone at an arranged place and time.
  *Example:* "I **met** my flatmate at an English school. Let's **meet** at the station at 6."
- **sell** – to give something to someone in exchange for money.
  *Example:* "I've **sold** my old bike on Facebook Marketplace for $80."
- **carry** – to hold something and take it with you from one place to another. (Spelling: consonant + y → *-ied*: carried.)
  *Example:* "Could you help me **carry** these boxes upstairs? I **carried** them all from the car."
- **fix** – to repair something that is broken; also to arrange or decide a time/price.
  *Example:* "The landlord still hasn't **fixed** the washing machine. He's **fixing** it on Monday."
