# Daily English – 2026-11-27

## Phrasal verb of the day: **Turn down**
**Meaning:**
1. To make the volume, heat or light lower. (ES: bajar el volumen / la temperatura)
2. To say "no" to an offer, invitation or request. (ES: rechazar)

**Context:** at home or in a share house (music, TV, aircon), at work (job offers, shifts), with friends (invitations).
- "Can you **turn down** the music? My housemate is sleeping."
- "It's freezing in here – let's **turn** the aircon **down**."
- "They offered me extra shifts, but I **turned** them **down** because of my English classes."
- "She **turned down** the job in Melbourne and stayed in Sydney."

> Tip to remember: imagine turning a volume knob **down** → less sound, or a "no" → the offer goes **down**. Opposite: **turn up** (louder).

## Verbs of the day

| Verb | Type | Present (I / he) | Past simple | Past participle | -ing |
|------|------|------------------|-------------|-----------------|------|
| **make** | irregular | make / makes | **made** | **made** | making |
| **work** | regular | work / works | worked | worked | working |

### make (irregular) – to create, prepare or produce something. (ES: hacer, preparar)
- Present: "I **make** a coffee every morning before class."
- Past: "I **made** pasta for my housemates last night."
- Present perfect: "Have you **made** any Aussie friends yet?"
- Continuous: "Shh, I'm **making** a phone call."
- Future: "I **will make** a booking for Saturday."

> Common combos: make a coffee, make friends, make money, make a mistake, make a decision.

### work (regular) – to do a job; or for something to function correctly. (ES: trabajar / funcionar)
- Present: "I **work** at a café in Bondi."
- Past: "I **worked** eight hours yesterday."
- Present perfect: "I **have worked** here for three months."
- Continuous: "She's **working** tonight, so she can't come."
- Future: "I **will work** on Sunday."
- Function: "My phone doesn't **work** – I need to top it up."

## Mini-practice (one sentence using all three)
"I **worked** all day, then I **made** dinner and **turned down** an invitation to go out because I was tired."

## Phrasal verb of the day: **Sort out**
**Meaning:** to fix a problem, organise something, or deal with a situation. (ES: solucionar, arreglar, organizar)
**Context:** very common in Australia – bills, paperwork, problems at work, plans with friends, a messy room.
- "Don't worry about the bank issue, I'll **sort it out** tomorrow."
- "I need to **sort out** my tax file number before I start the new job."
- "We **sorted out** the plans for Saturday – we're going to the beach."
- "Is everything **sorted**?" – "Yeah, all **sorted**!" (Aussie short form = "all fixed / ready")

> Tip to remember: *sort* = put things in order → **sort out** = put a problem in order.

# Daily English – 2026-11-25

## Phrasal verb of the day: **Sort out**

**Meaning:** to fix a problem, organise something, or make a situation OK. → *resolver / arreglar / organizar*

**Contexts (very Aussie!):** problems at work, paperwork (visa, bank, phone plan), messy rooms, plans with friends.

- "Don't worry, I'll **sort it out** tomorrow." (fix a problem)

- "Let's **sort out** the plans for Saturday." (decide/organise)

**Tip to remember:** *sort* = put in order → *sort out* = put things in order until the problem disappears.
**Grammar note:** with a pronoun it goes in the middle: "sort **it** out" (not "sort out it").

## Verbs of the day

|------|------|---------|-------------|-----------------|------|

- Past simple: "I **found** my wallet under the car seat."
- Present perfect: "Have you **found** a job yet?"
- Future: "I **will find** a cheaper room next month."

**Remember:** find – found – found (rhymes with *round*, *sound*).

**Remember:** always **wait for** someone/something ("wait for the bus", not "wait the bus"). Regular: just add **-ed** → waited.

## Phrasal verb: Pick up

**Meaning (3 common uses):**
1. To lift something from a surface. → *levantar / recoger algo*
2. To collect someone or something from a place (often by car). → *recoger a alguien*
3. To learn something informally, just by hearing or doing it. → *aprender sin estudiar*

**Contexts:** everyday life, transport, shopping, learning English in Australia.

- "Can you **pick up** my keys? They fell under the table." (1)
- "I'll **pick you up** from the station at 6." (2)
- "I need to **pick up** some bread from Coles on the way home." (2)
- "I've **picked up** a lot of Aussie slang at work." (3)

**Tip to remember:** *pick* = choose/take + *up* = towards you → you take something (or someone) to you.
**Grammar note:** with a pronoun, it goes in the middle: "pick **me** up", "pick **it** up" (not "pick up me").

- Present continuous: "I'm **driving** right now, I'll call you later."
- Past simple: "We **drove** to the Blue Mountains last weekend."
- Present perfect: "Have you ever **driven** on the left side of the road?"
- Future: "I **will drive** you to the airport tomorrow."

**Remember:** drive – drove – driven (like *ride – rode – ridden*).

### book (regular) – to reserve a table, ticket, room, appointment. → *reservar*

**Meaning:** to add more of something so it's full again, usually money or credit (or a drink).

# Daily English – 2026-11-21

## Phrasal verb: Look up

**Meaning:**
1. To search for information (in a dictionary, on Google, on a map, in an app).
2. *(look something up)* – the object can go in the middle: "look **it** up".
3. *(things are looking up)* – a situation is getting better.

**Contexts:** studying English, checking bus/train times, finding a place, looking for a job, talking about life improving.

**Examples:**
- "I didn't know the word 'arvo', so I **looked it up**." (arvo = afternoon 🇦🇺)
- "Can you **look up** the next train to the city?"
- "**Look up** the opening hours before you go – shops close early on Sunday."
- "I found a new job and a nice room. Things are **looking up**!"

**Easy to remember:** when you don't know something, you *look up* the answer. 🔍

## Verbs

| Verb | Type | Present (I / he) | Past simple | Past participle | -ing |
|------|------|------------------|-------------|-----------------|------|
| drink | irregular | drink / drinks | **drank** | **drunk** | drinking |
| cancel | regular | cancel / cancels | **cancelled** | **cancelled** | cancelling |

### drink (irregular) – to put a liquid in your mouth and swallow it
Memory trick: **i → a → u** → drink, drank, drunk (same as *swim, swam, swum*).
- Present: "I **drink** a flat white every morning."
- Past: "We **drank** a few beers at the pub last night."
- Present perfect: "Have you ever **drunk** kombucha?"
- Continuous: "She's **drinking** water because it's so hot today."
- Future: "I'll **drink** some tea before bed."

### cancel (regular) – to say that something planned will not happen
Note: in Australian/British English the "l" doubles → cancel**led**, cancel**ling** (US: canceled).
- Present: "I always **cancel** my gym membership in winter."
- Past: "They **cancelled** the BBQ because of the rain."
- Present perfect: "My flight **has been cancelled**!"
- Continuous: "I'm **cancelling** my Netflix subscription to save money."
- Future: "If it storms, they **will cancel** the concert."

# Daily English – 2026-11-20

## Phrasal verbs

### 1. Find out
**Meaning:** to discover a fact or piece of information, often by asking, reading or checking.
**Context:** timetables, prices, news, results, information about someone. (Use *find* for a physical object; *find out* for information.)
- "Can you **find out** what time the last train leaves Central?"
- "I just **found out** my visa application was approved!"
- "She went online to **find out** how much a rental bond costs."

### 2. Hold on
**Meaning:** (a) to wait for a short time; (b) to grip something tightly so you don't fall.
**Context:** phone calls, conversations, customer service, public transport (buses, trams).
- "**Hold on** a sec, I'll grab my wallet." (very common with *a sec / a minute*)
- "Please **hold on** while I transfer your call."
- "**Hold on** tight — the bus driver brakes hard on this road!"

### 3. Head off
**Meaning:** to leave a place to go somewhere else. Very common in Australian English.
**Context:** saying goodbye at work, parties, the pub, starting a trip.
- "Right, I'm gonna **head off** now — see you tomorrow!"
- "We're **heading off** to the beach early, around 7."
- "What time did they **head off** last night?"

## Verbs

| Verb | Type | Present | Past simple | Past participle | -ing form |
|------|------|---------|-------------|-----------------|-----------|
| meet | irregular | meet / meets | met | met | meeting |
| sell | irregular | sell / sells | sold | sold | selling |
| carry | regular | carry / carries | carried | carried | carrying |
| fix | regular | fix / fixes | fixed | fixed | fixing |

- **meet** – (a) to see and talk to someone for the first time; (b) to come together with someone at an arranged place and time.

### Lend (irregular)
**Meaning:** to give something to someone temporarily (you are the owner).
- "Could you **lend** me your charger? Mine is dead."
- "She has **lent** me her car twice this month."

### Borrow (regular)
**Meaning:** to take something from someone temporarily (they are the owner). Opposite direction of *lend*.
- "Can I **borrow** your umbrella? It's pouring outside."

### Book (regular)
**Meaning:** to reserve something in advance (table, ticket, appointment, room).
- "I've **booked** a table for four at 7:30."

# Daily English – 2026-11-14

## Phrasal verbs

### 1. Fill in / Fill out
**Meaning:** to complete a form or document by writing the information needed.
**Context:** forms at the bank, Medicare, job applications, rental applications, visa paperwork. In Australia "fill in" is more common; "fill out" is more American, but both are understood.
- "Can you fill in this form and bring it back to reception?"
- "I filled out the rental application online last night."
- Extra meaning – **fill (someone) in** = to tell someone the latest news: "I missed the meeting, can you fill me in?"

### 2. Look forward to
**Meaning:** to feel happy and excited about something that is going to happen.
**Context:** plans, holidays, events, and very often at the end of emails. ⚠️ It's followed by a noun or a verb + **-ing** (not the infinitive).
- "I'm really looking forward to the weekend."
- "I look forward to hearing from you." (formal email ending)
- "We're looking forward to going to the beach on Sunday."

### 3. Hang out (with)
**Meaning:** to spend relaxed, free time with someone or somewhere, without a special plan.
**Context:** friends, social life, weekends – very casual and common in Australia.
- "Do you want to hang out after class?"
- "I usually hang out with my housemates on Friday nights."
- "We hung out at the park all afternoon."
