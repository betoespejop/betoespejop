# Daily English – 2026-11-24

## Phrasal verb: Pick up

**Meaning (3 common uses):**
1. To lift something from a surface. → *levantar / recoger algo*
2. To collect someone or something from a place (often by car). → *recoger a alguien*
3. To learn something informally, just by hearing or doing it. → *aprender sin estudiar*

**Contexts:** everyday life, transport, shopping, learning English in Australia.

- "Can you **pick up** my keys? They fell under the table." (1)
- "I'll **pick you up** from the station at 6." (2)
- "I need to **pick up** some bread from Coles on the way home." (2)
- "I've **picked up** a lot of Aussie slang at work." (3)

**Tip to remember:** *pick* = choose/take + *up* = towards you → you take something (or someone) to you.
**Grammar note:** with a pronoun, it goes in the middle: "pick **me** up", "pick **it** up" (not "pick up me").

## Verbs

| Verb | Type | Present | Past simple | Past participle | -ing |
|------|------|---------|-------------|-----------------|------|
| drive | irregular | drive / drives | drove | driven | driving |
| book | regular | book / books | booked | booked | booking |

### drive (irregular) – to control a car, bus, etc. → *conducir / manejar*
- Present simple: "I **drive** to work every day."
- Present continuous: "I'm **driving** right now, I'll call you later."
- Past simple: "We **drove** to the Blue Mountains last weekend."
- Present perfect: "Have you ever **driven** on the left side of the road?"
- Future: "I **will drive** you to the airport tomorrow."

**Remember:** drive – drove – driven (like *ride – rode – ridden*).

### book (regular) – to reserve a table, ticket, room, appointment. → *reservar*
- Present simple: "She always **books** her flights online."
- Present continuous: "I'm **booking** a table for Friday night."
- Past simple: "I **booked** a doctor's appointment for Monday."
- Present perfect: "Have you **booked** the hostel yet?"
- Future: "I'**ll book** the tickets tonight."

**Remember:** a *book* is something you read, but "to book" = to reserve. Regular: just add **-ed** → booked.

## Mini challenge
Make one sentence using all three: *pick up + drive + book*.
Example: "I **booked** a car, so I'll **drive** to your place and **pick you up** at 8."

# Daily English – 2026-11-11

## Phrasal verbs

### 1. Run out (of)
**Meaning:** to use all of something so there's none left.
**Context:** shopping, money, time, petrol, phone battery.
- "We've run out of milk – can you grab some from Woolies?"
- "I'm running out of time to finish my assignment."
- "My phone ran out of battery on the train."

### 2. Figure out
**Meaning:** to understand or solve something after thinking about it.
**Context:** problems, directions, how something works, decisions.
- "I can't figure out how to top up my Opal card."
- "Give me a minute, I'll figure it out."
- "She finally figured out which bus goes to the beach."

### 3. Catch up (with)
**Meaning:** (a) to meet a friend and talk about recent news; (b) to reach the same level as others.
**Context:** social life (very common in Australia!), work, study.
- "Let's catch up for a coffee this weekend." (a)
- "It was great to catch up with you!" (a)
- "I missed two classes, so I need to catch up." (b)

## Verbs

| Verb | Type | Past simple | Past participle | -ing |
|------|------|-------------|-----------------|------|
| bring | irregular | brought | brought | bringing |
| pay | irregular | paid | paid | paying |
| rent | regular | rented | rented | renting |
| apply | regular | applied | applied | applying |

- **bring** – to carry something/someone to the place where you are going.
  "Can you bring a plate of food to the barbie on Saturday?" / "I brought my lunch from home."
- **pay** – to give money for something.
  "I paid the electricity bill yesterday." / "Have you paid the rent yet?"
- **rent** – to pay money regularly to use a house, room, car, etc.
  "I'm renting a room in a share house in Bondi." / "We rented a car for the road trip."
- **apply (for)** – to formally ask for a job, visa, course, etc., usually in writing.
  "I applied for three jobs this week." / "She has applied for a working holiday visa."
