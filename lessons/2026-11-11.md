# Daily English – 2026-11-11

## Phrasal verbs

### 1. Run out (of)
**Meaning:** to use all of something so there's none left.
**Context:** shopping, money, time, petrol, phone battery.
- "We've run out of milk – can you grab some from Woolies?"
- "I'm running out of time to finish my assignment."
- "My phone ran out of battery on the train."

### 2. Figure out
**Meaning:** to understand or solve something after thinking about it.
**Context:** problems, directions, how something works, decisions.
- "I can't figure out how to top up my Opal card."
- "Give me a minute, I'll figure it out."
- "She finally figured out which bus goes to the beach."

### 3. Catch up (with)
**Meaning:** (a) to meet a friend and talk about recent news; (b) to reach the same level as others.
**Context:** social life (very common in Australia!), work, study.
- "Let's catch up for a coffee this weekend." (a)
- "It was great to catch up with you!" (a)
- "I missed two classes, so I need to catch up." (b)

## Verbs

| Verb | Type | Past simple | Past participle | -ing |
|------|------|-------------|-----------------|------|
| bring | irregular | brought | brought | bringing |
| pay | irregular | paid | paid | paying |
| rent | regular | rented | rented | renting |
| apply | regular | applied | applied | applying |

- **bring** – to carry something/someone to the place where you are going.
  "Can you bring a plate of food to the barbie on Saturday?" / "I brought my lunch from home."
- **pay** – to give money for something.
  "I paid the electricity bill yesterday." / "Have you paid the rent yet?"
- **rent** – to pay money regularly to use a house, room, car, etc.
  "I'm renting a room in a share house in Bondi." / "We rented a car for the road trip."
- **apply (for)** – to formally ask for a job, visa, course, etc., usually in writing.
  "I applied for three jobs this week." / "She has applied for a working holiday visa."
