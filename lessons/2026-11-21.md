# Daily English – 2026-11-21

## Phrasal verb: Look up

**Meaning:**
1. To search for information (in a dictionary, on Google, on a map, in an app).
2. *(look something up)* – the object can go in the middle: "look **it** up".
3. *(things are looking up)* – a situation is getting better.

**Contexts:** studying English, checking bus/train times, finding a place, looking for a job, talking about life improving.

**Examples:**
- "I didn't know the word 'arvo', so I **looked it up**." (arvo = afternoon 🇦🇺)
- "Can you **look up** the next train to the city?"
- "**Look up** the opening hours before you go – shops close early on Sunday."
- "I found a new job and a nice room. Things are **looking up**!"

**Easy to remember:** when you don't know something, you *look up* the answer. 🔍

### drink (irregular) – to put a liquid in your mouth and swallow it
Memory trick: **i → a → u** → drink, drank, drunk (same as *swim, swam, swum*).
- Present: "I **drink** a flat white every morning."
- Past: "We **drank** a few beers at the pub last night."
- Present perfect: "Have you ever **drunk** kombucha?"
- Continuous: "She's **drinking** water because it's so hot today."
- Future: "I'll **drink** some tea before bed."

### cancel (regular) – to say that something planned will not happen
Note: in Australian/British English the "l" doubles → cancel**led**, cancel**ling** (US: canceled).
- Present: "I always **cancel** my gym membership in winter."
- Past: "They **cancelled** the BBQ because of the rain."
- Present perfect: "My flight **has been cancelled**!"
- Continuous: "I'm **cancelling** my Netflix subscription to save money."
- Future: "If it storms, they **will cancel** the concert."
