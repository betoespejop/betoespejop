# Daily English – 2026-11-16

## Phrasal verbs

### 1. Give up
**Meaning:** (a) to stop doing something, especially a habit; (b) to stop trying because something is too hard.
**Context:** habits (smoking, sugar), goals, learning, difficult tasks.
- "I gave up coffee for a month – it was really hard!" (a)
- "Don't give up on your English – you're improving every day." (b)
- "He tried to fix the car himself but gave up after an hour." (b)

### 2. Get along (with)
**Meaning:** to have a friendly relationship with someone.
**Context:** flatmates, coworkers, family, classmates.
- "I get along really well with my flatmates."
- "Do you get along with your new boss?"
- "My sister and I didn't get along when we were kids."

### 3. Check out
**Meaning:** (a) to look at something interesting / visit a place; (b) to leave a hotel/hostel after paying.
**Context:** recommendations, places to visit, travel, accommodation.
- "You should check out the markets in Fremantle – they're awesome." (a)
- "Check out this video, it's hilarious!" (a)
- "We have to check out of the hostel by 10 am." (b)

## Verbs

| Verb | Type | Past simple | Past participle | -ing |
|------|------|-------------|-----------------|------|
| buy | irregular | bought | bought | buying |
| drive | irregular | drove | driven | driving |
| cook | regular | cooked | cooked | cooking |
| try | regular | tried | tried | trying |

- **buy** – to get something by paying money for it.
  "I bought a second-hand bike on Facebook Marketplace." / "Have you bought your ticket yet?"
- **drive** – to control a car or other vehicle; also to take someone somewhere by car.
  "In Australia people drive on the left." / "My friend drove me to the airport." / "I've never driven a ute before."
- **cook** – to prepare food by heating it.
  "I cooked pasta for my flatmates last night." / "We're cooking snags on the barbie this arvo."
- **try** – (a) to make an effort to do something; (b) to test or taste something new.
  "I'm trying to speak English all day, even at home." / "Have you ever tried Vegemite?" / "She tried on three jackets before buying one."
  *Note: try → tried (y changes to i), but trying keeps the y.*
