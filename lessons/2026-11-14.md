# Daily English – 2026-11-14

## Phrasal verbs

### 1. Fill in / Fill out
**Meaning:** to complete a form or document by writing the information needed.
**Context:** forms at the bank, Medicare, job applications, rental applications, visa paperwork. In Australia "fill in" is more common; "fill out" is more American, but both are understood.
- "Can you fill in this form and bring it back to reception?"
- "I filled out the rental application online last night."
- Extra meaning – **fill (someone) in** = to tell someone the latest news: "I missed the meeting, can you fill me in?"

### 2. Look forward to
**Meaning:** to feel happy and excited about something that is going to happen.
**Context:** plans, holidays, events, and very often at the end of emails. ⚠️ It's followed by a noun or a verb + **-ing** (not the infinitive).
- "I'm really looking forward to the weekend."
- "I look forward to hearing from you." (formal email ending)
- "We're looking forward to going to the beach on Sunday."

### 3. Hang out (with)
**Meaning:** to spend relaxed, free time with someone or somewhere, without a special plan.
**Context:** friends, social life, weekends – very casual and common in Australia.
- "Do you want to hang out after class?"
- "I usually hang out with my housemates on Friday nights."
- "We hung out at the park all afternoon."

## Verbs

| Verb | Type | Past simple | Past participle | -ing |
|------|------|-------------|-----------------|------|
| take | irregular | took | taken | taking |
| leave | irregular | left | left | leaving |
| borrow | regular | borrowed | borrowed | borrowing |
| share | regular | shared | shared | sharing |

- **take** – to get or carry something; also to use transport or to need an amount of time.
  "I take the train to work every day." / "It took me 40 minutes to get to the city."
- **leave** – to go away from a place; also to let something stay somewhere (sometimes by mistake).
  "What time do you leave work?" / "Oh no, I've left my wallet at home!"
- **borrow** – to take and use something that belongs to someone else, and give it back later. (Opposite: *lend* = to give.)
  "Can I borrow your charger for a minute?" / "I borrowed a book from the library."
- **share** – to have or use something together with other people; also to tell others your ideas or feelings.
  "I'm sharing a flat with three other students." / "Thanks for sharing your story with us."
