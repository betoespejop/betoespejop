# Daily English – 2026-11-17

## Phrasal verbs

### 1. Chip in
**Meaning:** to give a small amount of money (or help) as part of a group, so everyone contributes.
**Context:** group gifts, shared bills, house expenses, BBQs, work collections. Very common in Australia.
- "We're all chipping in $10 for Sarah's birthday present."
- "If everyone chips in, we can afford a bigger Airbnb."
- "Can you chip in with the cleaning this weekend?" (help, not money)

### 2. Set up
**Meaning:** (a) to prepare or install something so it's ready to use; (b) to start or organise something (a business, a meeting, an account).
**Context:** technology, new home, work, banking, events.
- "I need to set up my new phone and bank account." (a)
- "Can you help me set up the tables for the party?" (a)
- "My manager set up a meeting with the new client for Friday." (b)

### 3. Come across
**Meaning:** (a) to find or meet something/someone by chance; (b) to seem / give an impression (come across as…).
**Context:** discoveries, reading, first impressions, job interviews.
- "I came across a great Colombian café near the beach." (a)
- "If you come across any cheap rooms, let me know." (a)
- "He comes across as shy, but he's really friendly once you know him." (b)

## Verbs

| Verb | Type | Present (he/she) | Past simple | Past participle | -ing |
|------|------|------------------|-------------|-----------------|------|
| catch | irregular | catches | caught | caught | catching |
| wear | irregular | wears | wore | worn | wearing |
| order | regular | orders | ordered | ordered | ordering |
| clean | regular | cleans | cleaned | cleaned | cleaning |

- **catch** – to take a bus, train, ferry or plane; also to get an illness or to grab something moving.
  "I usually catch the 7:15 train to work." / "I caught a cold last week." / "Have you ever caught a fish?"
- **wear** – to have clothes, shoes, glasses, etc. on your body.
  "Don't forget to wear sunscreen and a hat – the UV is extreme today." / "She wore a dress to the wedding." / "I've worn these shoes every day this month."
- **order** – to ask for food, drinks or products to be brought or sent to you.
  "Can I order a flat white, please?" / "We ordered pizza last night." / "I'm ordering a new charger online."
- **clean** – to remove dirt from something; to make it tidy.
  "I clean the kitchen every Sunday." / "My housemate cleaned the bathroom yesterday." / "Have you cleaned your room yet? The inspection is tomorrow!"
