# Daily English – 2026-11-27

## Phrasal verb of the day: **Turn down**
**Meaning:**
1. To make the volume, heat or light lower. (ES: bajar el volumen / la temperatura)
2. To say "no" to an offer, invitation or request. (ES: rechazar)

**Context:** at home or in a share house (music, TV, aircon), at work (job offers, shifts), with friends (invitations).
- "Can you **turn down** the music? My housemate is sleeping."
- "It's freezing in here – let's **turn** the aircon **down**."
- "They offered me extra shifts, but I **turned** them **down** because of my English classes."
- "She **turned down** the job in Melbourne and stayed in Sydney."

> Tip to remember: imagine turning a volume knob **down** → less sound, or a "no" → the offer goes **down**. Opposite: **turn up** (louder).

## Verbs of the day

| Verb | Type | Present (I / he) | Past simple | Past participle | -ing |
|------|------|------------------|-------------|-----------------|------|
| **make** | irregular | make / makes | **made** | **made** | making |
| **work** | regular | work / works | worked | worked | working |

### make (irregular) – to create, prepare or produce something. (ES: hacer, preparar)
- Present: "I **make** a coffee every morning before class."
- Past: "I **made** pasta for my housemates last night."
- Present perfect: "Have you **made** any Aussie friends yet?"
- Continuous: "Shh, I'm **making** a phone call."
- Future: "I **will make** a booking for Saturday."

> Common combos: make a coffee, make friends, make money, make a mistake, make a decision.

### work (regular) – to do a job; or for something to function correctly. (ES: trabajar / funcionar)
- Present: "I **work** at a café in Bondi."
- Past: "I **worked** eight hours yesterday."
- Present perfect: "I **have worked** here for three months."
- Continuous: "She's **working** tonight, so she can't come."
- Future: "I **will work** on Sunday."
- Function: "My phone doesn't **work** – I need to top it up."
