# Daily English – 2026-11-19

## Phrasal verbs

### 1. Get on / Get off
**Meaning:** to enter (get on) or leave (get off) a bus, train, tram, ferry, plane or bike.
**Context:** public transport, travel. (For cars and taxis, say *get in / get out of*.)
- "Get on the 333 at Central and get off at Bondi Beach."
- "Excuse me, which stop should I get off at for the hospital?"
- "Tap your Opal card when you get on and when you get off."

### 2. Put up with
**Meaning:** to accept or tolerate something or someone annoying without complaining.
**Context:** housemates, noise, bad weather, difficult bosses or customers.
- "I can't put up with my housemate's dirty dishes any longer."
- "How do you put up with the flies in summer?"
- "She put up with a long commute because the rent was cheap."

### 3. Run into
**Meaning:** (a) to meet someone by chance, without planning it; (b) to experience a problem unexpectedly.
**Context:** social life, around town; work/study problems.
- "I ran into my English teacher at the supermarket yesterday." (a)
- "Guess who I ran into at the beach? Carlos from class!" (a)
- "We ran into some problems with the visa application." (b)

## Verbs

| Verb | Type | 3rd person | Past simple | Past participle | -ing |
|------|------|------------|-------------|-----------------|------|
| lose | irregular | loses | lost | lost | losing |
| forget | irregular | forgets | forgot | forgotten | forgetting |
| ask | regular | asks | asked | asked | asking |
| change | regular | changes | changed | changed | changing |

- **lose** – to not be able to find something; to stop having something; to not win.
  Present: "I always lose my keys." · Past: "I lost my wallet on the bus." · Present perfect: "Have you ever lost your phone?" · Continuous: "We're losing the game 2–0."
- **forget** – to not remember something.
  Present: "I sometimes forget people's names." · Past: "I forgot to bring my umbrella." · Present perfect: "Sorry, I've forgotten your name." · Continuous: "I keep forgetting my password."
- **ask** – to say something to get information, or to request something.
  Present: "I usually ask the barista for an oat flat white." · Past: "I asked my boss for a day off." · Present perfect: "Have you asked the landlord about the leak?" · Continuous: "She's asking about the job."
- **change** – to make or become different; to replace one thing with another; to swap clothes or trains.
  Present: "The weather in Melbourne changes every hour!" · Past: "I changed my shift to Saturday." · Present perfect: "Prices have changed a lot this year." · Continuous: "I'm changing trains at Town Hall."
