# Daily English – 2026-11-28

## Phrasal verb: Sort out
**Meaning:** to fix a problem or organise something so it's no longer messy or confusing.
**Easy way to remember:** "sort" = put in order → *sort out* = put the problem in order.
**Context:** problems at work, bills, paperwork (visa, bank, phone plan), a messy room, plans with friends. Very common in Australia.
- "Don't worry, I'll sort it out." (I'll fix it)
- "I need to sort out my tax file number this week."
- "We sorted out the problem with the landlord."
- "Let's sort out the plans for Saturday."

## Verbs

| Verb | Type | Present (he/she) | Past simple | Past participle | -ing |
|------|------|------------------|-------------|-----------------|------|
| find | irregular | finds | found | found | finding |
| book | regular | books | booked | booked | booking |

### find – to discover something you were looking for (or by chance)
- Present simple: "I always **find** good deals at Kmart."
- Present continuous: "I'm **finding** it hard to understand the Aussie accent."
- Past simple: "I **found** my keys under the couch."
- Present perfect: "Have you **found** a job yet?"
- Future: "I **will find** a cheaper room next month."
**Remember:** find → found → found ("I *found* it!")

### book – to reserve a place, ticket, table or appointment in advance
- Present simple: "She **books** her flights online."
- Present continuous: "I'm **booking** a table for four at 7."
- Past simple: "I **booked** a doctor's appointment for Monday."
- Present perfect: "We've **booked** the tickets to Melbourne."
- Future: "I'**ll book** a haircut for Friday."
**Remember:** regular = just add **-ed** → booked (pronounced "bookt").
